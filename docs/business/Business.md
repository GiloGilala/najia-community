## 1. Executive Summary

Najia Community Bridge is a non-profit civic technology platform. The financial goal is **sustainability, not profit**. By the end of Year 3, the platform should cover its operating costs from a diversified revenue base (lawyer listings, government/NGO poll fees, training, grants) with no structural reliance on a single funder.

| Metric | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Total available funding | ₦22.0M | ₦25.0M | ₦30.0M |
| Operating costs | ₦25.0M | ₦27.5M | ₦30.0M |
| **Funding gap** | **₦3.0M** | **₦2.5M** | **₦0** |
| Break-even | No | No (approaching) | On paper (no margin) |
| Primary funding source | Grants | Mixed | Mixed |

**The single most important financial fact:** Year 1 has a ₦3M gap that must be closed before the Lagos pilot launches. This is a hard gate, not a soft target. The detailed closure plan is in §7.

## 2. Business Model

### 2.1 Mission-Aligned Business Model

The platform operates as a mission-driven, non-profit-aligned organization. The financial model must be:

- **Self-sustaining by Year 3** — no permanent reliance on a single grant
- **Independent** — no revenue stream that creates a conflict of interest with the mission (e.g., no political advertising, no behavioral targeting, no data sales)
- **Aligned with legal/regulatory constraints** — particularly the Bar Association's prohibition on fee-splitting with non-lawyers (see §5.4)

### 2.2 Value Exchange

| Stakeholder | What They Give | What They Get |
|-------------|----------------|---------------|
| Citizens | Verified identity, time, data (anonymized) | Voice in governance, evidence security, legal access, civic education |
| Lawyers | Listing fee, professional time (incl. pro bono) | Client acquisition, professional visibility, pro bono recognition |
| Government / NGOs | Poll fees (where applicable) | Real-time sentiment data, structured feedback |
| Donors / grant funders | Cash grants, in-kind support | Measurable social impact, transparency reports |
| Content contributors | Writing, expertise | Authorship credit, audience reach |

### 2.3 What the Platform Will NOT Do (Revenue)

These are codified in PLATFORM.md §10.1.2 and reiterated here because they are foundational to the business model:

- ❌ Sell citizen data
- ❌ Run behavioral advertising
- ❌ Take a percentage of legal fees (Bar Association fee-splitting prohibition)
- ❌ Charge citizens access fees (would exclude low-income users)
- ❌ Accept political advocacy funding without explicit transparency disclosure

| Stream | Description | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | Feasibility |

| **Government / NGO polls** | Fee for government agencies or NGOs to commission a poll | ₦0.5M | ₦3.0M | ₦5.0M | High |
| **Training & certification** | Legal literacy courses with certificate fees | ₦0.0M | ₦1.0M | ₦1.5M | Low–Medium |
| **Premium verification** | Expedited evidence verification, certificate issuance | ₦0.0M | ₦0.5M | ₦0.4M | Medium |
| **Sponsored content** | Educational content from trusted partners, clearly labeled | ₦0.0M | ₦0.0M | ₦0.1M | Low |
| **Data products** | Anonymized trend reports for policy organizations | ₦0.5M | ₦0.5M | ₦0.0M | Medium |
| **Subtotal (revenue)** | | **₦2.0M** | **₦15.0M** | **₦25.0M** | |
| **Grant funding** | Donor support for civic engagement and access to justice | ₦20.0M | ₦10.0M | ₦5.0M | High (Year 1), Medium (Year 3) |

### 3.2 Revenue Stream Detail

#### 3.2.1 Lawyer Listings (Primary revenue stream from Year 2)

- **Model:** Monthly subscription per lawyer profile. Tiered (basic / enhanced / premium).
- **Pricing targets (initial):** Basic ₦3,000/mo, Enhanced ₦7,000/mo, Premium ₦15,000/mo.
- **Volume targets:**

| Year | Active listings (avg) | Avg. revenue per listing/mo | Annual revenue |
|------|------------------------|------------------------------|----------------|
| 1 | 5 | ₦3,000 (basic only) | ₦180K |
| 2 | 50 | ₦5,000 (mostly enhanced) | ₦3.0M |
| 3 | 200 | ₦7,500 (mixed) | ₦18.0M |

- **Critical compliance:** This is a **flat subscription**, **not a percentage of legal fees**. See §5.4 for the Bar Association fee-splitting rule and why this is non-negotiable.

#### 3.2.2 Government / NGO Poll Fees

- **Model:** Fee per commissioned poll, set by the Advisory Board.
- **Pricing targets:** ₦100K–₦500K per poll, depending on scope (national vs. state vs. local).
- **Volume targets:** 5 polls in Year 1 (pilot scale), 15 in Year 2, 25 in Year 3.
- **Constraint:** All such polls are still non-binding and carry the same disclaimers. The fee is for the platform's delivery of the poll, not for any binding outcome.

#### 3.2.3 Grant Funding (Year 1 anchor)

- **Target mix for Year 1:**

| Funder type | Share | Notes |
|-------------|-------|-------|
| International civic-tech funders (e.g., Open Society, Ford Foundation, Luminate) | 60% | Most likely anchor funders |
| Nigerian philanthropic foundations | 25% | Higher friction, higher reputational value |
| Government / multilateral development grants | 15% | Possible conflict-of-interest; reviewed case by case |

- **Diversification rule:** No single funder may represent more than 40% of total grant funding in any year. This is a hard governance rule, not a soft target, and is enforced by the Board of Directors.

### 3.3 Revenue Assumptions and Risks

| Assumption | Confidence | If it fails... |
|------------|------------|----------------|
| Lagos pilot can attract 10+ lawyers at basic listing in Year 1 | Medium | Push basic listing price down; rely more on Year 1 grants |
| Government agencies will pay for polls | Low–Medium | Drop the stream; lean on grant funding |
| International funders will back the mission | High (precedent) | Pivot to Nigerian foundations, accept slower growth |
| Premium verification market exists | Low | Deprioritize; remove from model |
| Sponsored content can stay clearly labeled and trusted | Low | Do not pursue |

## 4. Cost Structure

### 4.1 Operating Cost Categories

| Category | Description | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|----------|-------------|--------|--------|--------|
| **Infrastructure** | VPS hosting, storage, CDN, backup | ₦1.5M | ₦2.5M | ₦3.5M |
| **Identity verification** | NIMC NVS API fees, Onfido fees | ₦0.8M | ₦1.5M | ₦2.0M |
| **AI detection** | API costs for deepfake detection | ₦0.5M | ₦1.0M | ₦1.5M |
| **Development** | Engineering team salaries and benefits | ₦12.0M | ₦12.0M | ₦13.0M |
| **Moderation** | Moderator compensation and tools | ₦2.0M | ₦3.0M | ₦3.5M |
| **Legal** | Legal counsel, compliance, Bar Association liaison | ₦2.5M | ₦2.0M | ₦2.0M |
| **Marketing** | User acquisition, outreach, education | ₦1.5M | ₦2.0M | ₦2.0M |
| **Operations** | Facilities, administrative costs | ₦1.5M | ₦1.5M | ₦1.5M |
| **Content** | Blog writers, editors, legal reviewers | ₦2.0M | ₦2.0M | ₦1.0M |
| **Contingency (5%)** | Reserve for unexpected costs | ₦0.7M | ₦0.0M | ₦0.0M |
| **Total operating costs** | | **₦25.0M** | **₦27.5M** | **₦30.0M** |

### 4.2 Cost Notes by Category

- **Development (largest line):** The founding engineering team is intentionally lean — fewer than 5 engineers through Year 1. The bulk of cost is salaries. This is the line most likely to come under pressure if grants underdeliver.
- **Moderation:** Scales with user volume. The Year 2 and Year 3 figures assume a hybrid of paid moderators and pro bono legal professionals (modeled in the platform doc §10.2.2).
- **Legal:** Front-loaded in Year 1 to cover Bar Association engagement, NDPR compliance, and the pre-launch legal review. Drops in Years 2 and 3 to a maintenance level.
- **AI detection:** Assumes a hybrid of open-source models (free) and commercial APIs for the highest-accuracy cases. The split is ~60% open-source / ~40% commercial in Year 1, shifting to ~40/60 by Year 3 as accuracy requirements tighten.
- **Contingency:** Only allocated in Year 1 because the funding gap is the de facto contingency in Years 2 and 3.

### 4.3 Cost Optimization Levers

In order of preference (least to most disruptive):

1. **Use open-source AI models** wherever accuracy is sufficient (see §4.2)
2. **Pro bono moderators** (especially lawyers) — modeled in Year 1
3. **Volunteer content reviewers** for blog and legal literacy modules
4. **Cost-share with civil society partners** for outreach
5. **Defer premium features** (premium verification, sponsored content)
6. **Defer headcount** — last resort; slows the roadmap

We do **not** optimize by reducing security, legal, or NDPR compliance spend. Those are non-discretionary.

## 5. Pricing and Unit Economics

### 5.1 Lawyer Listing Unit Economics

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Average revenue per lawyer per month (target Year 3) | ₦7,500 |
| Cost to serve a lawyer (matching, support, dispute resolution) | ~₦1,200/mo |
| **Contribution margin per lawyer** | **~₦6,300/mo (84%)** |
| CAC (customer acquisition cost) target | < ₦15,000 |
| Payback period target | < 4 months |

### 5.2 Poll Fee Unit Economics

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Average poll fee | ₦200K |
| Cost to deliver (drafting, review, moderation, reporting) | ~₦80K |
| **Contribution margin per poll** | **~₦120K (60%)** |
| Lead time (Advisory Board review + publication) | 14–21 days |

### 5.3 Free-Tier Economics

The platform is free for citizens. The marginal cost of a citizen user:

| Activity | Marginal cost |
|----------|---------------|
| Account | < ₦10 (verification + storage) |
| One vote | < ₦1 (DB + cache) |
| One evidence upload | ~₦50 (storage + AI detection) |
| One blog read | < ₦1 (CDN + cache) |

This makes the citizen side of the platform financially viable at any realistic scale, provided the lawyer marketplace and grant funding cover the fixed costs.

### 5.4 Bar Association Fee-Splitting Constraint

The Nigerian Bar Association Rules of Professional Conduct (and the corresponding rules in most Nigerian jurisdictions) **prohibit fee-splitting between lawyers and non-lawyers**. A platform that takes a percentage of a lawyer's fees from a client engagement is engaged in fee-splitting.

**Implication for the business model:**

- The platform **must not** take a percentage of legal fees, contingency fees, or any fee that flows from a lawyer–client engagement.
- The lawyer listing / subscription model (§3.2.1) is a flat fee paid by the lawyer for the platform service of being listed, matched, and reviewed. It is **not** tied to the fees the lawyer charges their clients.
- The free consultation is platform-funded and is a service to the citizen, not a fee-share with the lawyer.

**This constraint is the reason** the Prohibited Revenue list in PLATFORM.md §10.1.2 rules out any fee tied to a legal-fee percentage, and why the lawyer marketplace's risk register in PLATFORM.md §5.6.1 flags fee-splitting as a standing operational risk. Any change to the fee model must be reviewed by legal counsel and, if material, the Bar Association.

### 6.2 Year 2 — Growth

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Revenue | ₦15,000,000 |
| Grant funding | ₦10,000,000 |
| **Total available** | **₦25,000,000** |

**Interpretation:** Revenue grows 7.5×, grant funding is cut in half. The platform is approaching sustainability but is not there yet. The Year 2 gap is smaller and easier to close than Year 1's.

### 6.3 Year 3 — Sustainability

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Revenue | ₦25,000,000 |
| Grant funding | ₦5,000,000 |
| **Total available** | **₦30,000,000** |
| Operating costs | ₦30,000,000 |
| **Funding gap** | **₦0** |
| Break-even | Achieved on paper, with **no margin** |

**Critical interpretation:** Year 3 break-even is on paper only. There is no buffer for:
- A grant funder pulling out
- An unexpected legal cost (e.g., a regulatory inquiry)
- An NIMC API outage requiring a temporary shift to Onfido for everyone
- Higher-than-expected AI detection costs
- A moderation surge

The platform is not "sustainable" in Year 3 in the durable sense. It is "approaching sustainability" and should not be described as break-even in public communications until at least 6 months of operating cost are held in reserve. The conservative target is **Year 4 with 6 months of operating reserve**.

## 7. Funding Strategy and Gap Closure

### 7.1 The ₦3M Year 1 Gap — Closure Plan

The Year 1 gap is a hard gate before pilot launch. The closure options, in order of preference:

| Option | Amount | Probability | Tradeoff |
|--------|--------|-------------|----------|
| Additional anchor grant (international funder) | ₦5M+ | Medium | Best option; preserves mission |
| Second Nigerian foundation grant | ₦2–3M | Low–Medium | Higher friction but better optics |
| Founder bridge financing (repayable from Year 2 revenue) | ₦3M | High | Use only if grants stall; documented as a loan, not a grant |
| Defer Phase 2 features (AI detection, mobile app) by 3 months | ₦2M saved | High | Operational cost only; doesn't close the full gap |
| Reduce marketing and outreach | ₦1M saved | High | Slower user acquisition; risk to pilot success |

**Recommended approach:** Pursue the additional anchor grant as the primary path. If by Month 2 the grant pipeline is not on track, invoke founder bridge financing as a contingency, with a written loan agreement and Board approval. **Do not** launch the pilot without full funding.

### 7.2 Diversification Rules

| Rule | Threshold | Source |
|------|-----------|--------|
| No single funder may represent more than 40% of total grant funding in any year | Hard | Board policy |
| No single revenue stream may represent more than 60% of total revenue by Year 3 | Hard | Board policy |
| Operating reserve target | 6 months of operating cost | Target by Year 4 |
| Annual financial audit required | Yes, by independent auditor | Statutory + donor requirement |

### 7.3 Donor Reporting Commitments

The platform commits to:

- **Quarterly transparency reports** (PLATFORM.md §14.2.1) including a financial summary
- **Annual audited financial statements** published publicly
- **Per-grant reporting** as required by each funder's terms
- **Real-time notification** to anchor funders of any material financial event (funding shortfall, regulatory action, security incident with financial impact)

## 8. Sensitivity Analysis

How do the numbers change if key assumptions move by ±20%?

| Scenario | Revenue change | Cost change | Year 1 gap | Year 3 status |
|----------|----------------|-------------|------------|---------------|
| Base case | ₦2.0M | ₦25.0M | ₦3.0M | Break-even on paper |
| Lawyer listings 20% below target | ₦1.84M (-8%) | no change | ₦3.16M | ₦1.6M gap |
| Grant funding 20% below target | ₦16.0M | no change | **₦7.0M** | ₦5.0M gap |
| Operating costs 20% above plan | no change | ₦30.0M | **₦8.0M** | ₦6.0M gap |
| All three at -20% / +20% | ₦1.55M (-23%) | ₦30.0M | **₦9.45M** | ₦8.45M gap |
| Lawyer listings 20% above target | ₦2.2M (+10%) | no change | ₦2.8M | Surplus ₦2.2M |

**Worst case combined (-20% grants, +20% costs, -20% lawyer listings):** Year 1 gap balloons to ~₦9.5M. This is the planning case for our contingency, not the expected case, but it is the case we must be able to describe credibly to the Board and to funders.

## 9. Key Financial Risks

| Risk | Impact | Probability | Mitigation |
|------|--------|-------------|------------|
| Year 1 grant funding falls short of ₦20M target | ₦3–7M gap | Medium | Diversified grant pipeline; founder bridge contingency |
| Bar Association rule change reinterprets fee-splitting to cover our model | Loss of primary revenue stream | Low | Continuous NBA engagement; legal review of any fee-model change |
| NIMC NVS API outage forces shift to Onfido for all verifications | +₦1M–₦2M in Year 1 | Low | Onfido already integrated as fallback; cost modeled in contingency |
| AI detection costs grow faster than projected | +₦1M–₦3M over 3 years | Medium | Open-source model usage; rate limiting on detection calls |
| Government retaliation reduces poll fee revenue | -₦3M–₦5M in Year 2/3 | Low–Medium | Diversification; non-binding framing reduces this risk |
| Major security incident with financial impact | Variable, up to ₦5M | Low | Insurance, incident response plan, security review pre-launch |
| Operating reserve never reaches 6-month target | Sustainability risk | Medium | Board-level KPI; addressed in Year 4 plan |

## 11. Decision Triggers (When to Re-Plan)

The financial plan must be re-opened if any of the following occur:

| Trigger | Action |
|---------|--------|
| Year 1 grant funding falls below ₦16M (80% of target) by Month 6 | Invoke contingency plan; defer non-essential features |
| Year 2 revenue (non-grant) comes in below ₦10M (67% of target) by Q2 | Re-plan Year 2 budget; cut discretionary spend |
| Bar Association issues guidance affecting lawyer listings | Halt or restructure lawyer marketplace; legal review |
| Operating costs exceed budget by >15% in any quarter | Cost review; sponsor notification within 14 days |
| NIMC NVS API access is denied or significantly restricted | Re-plan identity verification strategy; communicate to donors |
| A funder representing >25% of grant funding gives notice of withdrawal | Activate contingency plan; communicate to Board within 7 days |
