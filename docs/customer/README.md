## For Different Audiences

### For the Pilot Cohort

If you're a pilot participant, you don't need to read any of these documents in full. The platform is designed to be self-explanatory.

**If you have a question:**
- Check the [FAQ](./FAQ.md) first (60+ questions, organized by topic)
- Search with Ctrl+F (or Cmd+F on Mac) for keywords

**If you want to learn how to do something:**
- Read the relevant section of the [User Guide](./User%20Guide.md)
- The guide is organized by user journey, not by feature, so you can find what you need quickly

**If something isn't working:**
- Check the [Troubleshooting](./Troubleshooting.md) guide first
- The guide is organized by symptom, with a consistent structure (Symptom → Likely Cause → Solution → If That Doesn't Work)

**If you need to contact us:**
- Email support@najiacommunitybridge.com
- For pilot-specific questions: pilot@najiacommunitybridge.com

### For the Press and Media

If you're a journalist, blogger, or media professional:

- **For the platform's mission and key facts:** see the [Marketing Framework](./Marketing.md) §1 (Positioning) and §2 (Key Messages)
- **For the press kit:** contact press@najiacommunitybridge.com
- **For interviews:** contact press@najiacommunitybridge.com (the Project Sponsor is the authorized spokesperson)
- **For the transparency reports:** see [../operations/Operations.md §9.3](../operations/Operations.md#93-the-transparency-report)

**Important:** All polls are non-binding expressions of citizen sentiment. They have no legal or electoral weight. Please include this disclaimer in any coverage of the platform's polls.

### For the Marketing Team

If you're writing or reviewing marketing material:

- **Start with the [Marketing Framework](./Marketing.md)** — the positioning, key messages, voice, audiences, and review checklist
- **Refer to the [User Guide](./User%20Guide.md)** — to ensure the user-facing documentation and the marketing material are consistent
- **Use the [Troubleshooting](./Troubleshooting.md) and [FAQ](./FAQ.md)** — to understand the common questions and concerns, and address them proactively in the marketing material

**The most important rules:**
1. The non-binding disclaimer must be prominent in any mention of polls
2. The anonymity commitment must be clear in any mention of voting
3. The free commitment must be clear in any mention of the lawyer consultation
4. The "what we don't say" list in the Marketing Framework §2.3 must be respected

### For the Content Team

If you're creating or editing the user-facing content:

- **Read the [User Guide](./User%20Guide.md) and the [FAQ](./FAQ.md)** — to ensure consistency in tone, terminology, and style
- **Follow the voice in the [Marketing Framework](./Marketing.md)** — warm, clear, empowering, honest, respectful
- **Use the Troubleshooting guide as a reference** — for the common issues users face

**The content update process:**
1. Identify a gap (a support ticket reveals a missing article, a feature changes, the policy changes)
2. Update the relevant document (User Guide, FAQ, or Troubleshooting)
3. Have the Operations Director and the Engineering Lead review for accuracy (if applicable)
4. Have the Legal Director review (if the change affects policies or commitments)
5. Publish the update
6. Notify the team (so support can reference the new content)

### For the Pilot Support Team

If you're providing support to the pilot cohort:

- **Use the [User Guide](./User%20Guide.md)** to understand the platform's features
- **Use the [FAQ](./FAQ.md)** to answer common questions
- **Use the [Troubleshooting](./Troubleshooting.md)** to help with common problems
- **Escalate to the runbooks** in [../operations/Runbooks.md](../operations/Runbooks.md) when an issue becomes a P1 or P2 incident

**The support process** is in [../operations/Support.md](../operations/Support.md). The support tickets are triaged by tier; the runbooks cover the most common scenarios.

## The Voice and Tone

The customer documents use a consistent voice and tone, defined in the [Marketing Framework §3](./Marketing.md).

**The voice in one sentence:** Warm, clear, empowering, honest, and respectful.

**The tone varies by context:** welcoming (onboarding), reassuring (privacy), calm (security incidents), humble (feedback), professional (press), direct (crisis), genuine (celebration), formal (legal/compliance).

**The top 5 words to avoid:** "simply" / "just" (condescending), "user" / "customer" (use "citizen"), "revolutionary" / "game-changing" (hyperbole), "guaranteed" / "the best" (overpromising), "voter" (implies electoral effect; use "citizen" or "participant").

## Maintenance

The customer documentation is maintained by the Content Lead. Updates happen:

- **When the platform changes** (a new feature, a policy change, a UI change)
- **When pilot feedback reveals a gap** (a support ticket exposes missing or unclear content)
- **When the Marketing Framework is updated** (the user-facing documents must reflect the same voice and key messages)
- **Quarterly** — a review of all customer documents for accuracy and freshness

The CI pipeline checks that the customer documents are kept in sync with the platform's policies. Drift between documents is a content failure that triggers a review.

## Appendix B: The Customer Documentation Glossary

- **AI detection** — an automated check that estimates the likelihood that an image or video was generated or manipulated by AI
- **Anonymized** — data that cannot be tied back to a specific person
- **Bar verification** — the process of verifying a lawyer's bar license with the Body of Benchers
- **Confidence vote** — a non-binding vote on whether you have confidence in an elected official
- **DSAR** — Data Subject Access Request (your right to request a copy of your data)
- **Evidence** — a file (image, video, audio, document) uploaded to the platform, with integrity verification
- **Match score breakdown** — a transparent explanation of why a lawyer was matched to your case
- **NDPR** — Nigeria Data Protection Regulation
- **NIN** — National Identification Number
- **NIMC** — National Identity Management Commission
- **Non-binding** — not having legal or electoral effect (all polls are non-binding)
- **Policy poll** — a non-binding vote on a government policy or initiative
- **SHA-256** — the cryptographic hash algorithm used for evidence integrity
- **Verification** — the process of confirming your identity (via NIN or document)
