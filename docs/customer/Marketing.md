## 1. Positioning

### 1.1 The One-Line Description

**"A civic engagement and access-to-justice platform for Nigerian citizens."**

This is the elevator pitch. It answers three questions:
- **What is it?** A platform (a digital product, not a service or organization)
- **Who is it for?** Nigerian citizens (the primary audience)
- **What does it do?** Civic engagement + access to justice (the two pillars)

### 1.2 The Expanded Description

**For citizens:** Najia Community Bridge helps you have a voice in governance (through non-binding polls and confidence votes), protect your evidence (with cryptographic verification), and find legal help (through a free 15-minute consultation with a verified lawyer). It's free, it's private, and your votes are anonymous.

**For lawyers:** Najia Community Bridge connects you with citizens who need your services in your practice areas — without the overhead of marketing. The platform takes a flat monthly subscription, not a percentage of your fees. You focus on the work; we focus on the matching.

**For civil society:** Najia Community Bridge provides a transparent, independent platform for civic engagement. All polls are non-binding; all results are public; all funding is disclosed in our quarterly transparency reports.

**For donors and funders:** Najia Community Bridge is a civic technology initiative committed to transparency, accountability, and impact. Our quarterly transparency reports detail our funding, our activities, and our metrics. We are funded by grants, not by political or commercial interests.

### 1.3 The Positioning Pillars

The platform's positioning rests on three pillars. Every marketing message should support at least one of these pillars.

#### 1.3.1 Pillar 1: Voice

**Your voice matters. We're building a platform where you can express it.**

- **What it means:** Citizens have a structured way to express their views on government policies and elected officials
- **What it doesn't mean:** The platform is not a voting tool that affects elections or legislation
- **Why it matters:** In a democracy, the space between elections is where most decisions are made. The platform fills that gap with non-binding citizen sentiment
- **The proof:** The platform's polls are reviewed by an independent Advisory Board; the results are public; the methodology is transparent

#### 1.3.2 Pillar 2: Evidence

**Your evidence is protected. We verify it cryptographically.**

- **What it means:** Files uploaded to the platform are cryptographically verified to prove they haven't been changed
- **What it doesn't mean:** The platform doesn't verify the truth of the content; it verifies the integrity of the file
- **Why it matters:** In disputes (landlord, employer, consumer, family), evidence is often challenged as "possibly edited." The platform's integrity check makes that challenge impossible
- **The proof:** SHA-256 hashing on every upload, re-verified on every access, with mismatch alerts

#### 1.3.3 Pillar 3: Access

**Legal help should be accessible. We connect you with verified lawyers for free.**

- **What it means:** Citizens can schedule a free 15-minute consultation with a verified lawyer
- **What it doesn't mean:** The platform doesn't provide legal advice and doesn't take a percentage of legal fees
- **Why it matters:** Legal help is often expensive and opaque. The platform makes it free and transparent
- **The proof:** Every lawyer is bar-verified; the free consultation is funded by the platform; the engagement (if any) is private and outside the platform

### 1.4 The Positioning Compared to Alternatives

The platform is **not**:
- A voting platform that affects elections (we're non-binding)
- A legal service that provides legal advice (we connect you with lawyers)
- A social media platform (we're focused on civic engagement and access to justice)
- A government platform (we're independent and non-partisan)

The platform **is**:
- A civic engagement tool that complements (not replaces) elections
- A marketplace that connects (not provides) legal services
- A focused platform with a clear mission (not a general-purpose platform)
- An independent organization (not affiliated with any political party or commercial interest)

### 1.5 The Mission Statement

**"To empower Nigerian citizens with tools for informed civic participation, verified evidence management, and access to legal representation — while maintaining the highest standards of integrity, transparency, and legal compliance."**

This is the formal mission statement. It appears in:
- The website footer
- The "About" page
- The quarterly transparency report
- The press kit
- The investor/funder materials

## 2. Key Messages

The key messages are the core statements the platform communicates. Every marketing material should incorporate at least one of these messages.

### 2.1 The Primary Messages (the Top 5)

These are the five messages that should appear in every marketing material, in some form.

#### 2.1.1 Message 1: Non-Binding

**"All polls are non-binding expressions of citizen sentiment. They have no legal or electoral weight."**

- **When to use:** Every mention of polls. Every time a poll is referenced. Every press release about a poll.
- **How to use:** As a prominent disclaimer, not a footnote. The message should be clear and immediate.
- **Why it matters:** This is the platform's most important message to the public. It prevents the misconception that the platform influences elections or legislation.

#### 2.1.2 Message 2: Anonymous

**"Your votes are completely anonymous. We do not store your identity with your vote."**

- **When to use:** Every mention of voting. Every privacy-related communication.
- **How to use:** As a clear commitment, not a technical detail. The user should understand that their privacy is protected.
- **Why it matters:** Fear of identification is a real barrier to civic participation. The anonymity commitment addresses this fear.

#### 2.1.3 Message 3: Free

**"The platform is free for citizens. The free 15-minute lawyer consultation is paid by the platform, not by you."**

- **When to use:** Every mention of the lawyer marketplace. Every mention of access to justice.
- **How to use:** As a clear commitment, not a marketing slogan. The user should understand that there are no hidden costs.
- **Why it matters:** Legal help is often perceived as expensive. The free commitment removes a barrier to access.

#### 2.1.4 Message 4: Verified

**"Every lawyer is bar-verified. Every evidence file is cryptographically verified. Every result is independently reviewed."**

- **When to use:** Every mention of lawyers. Every mention of evidence. Every mention of the Advisory Board.
- **How to use:** As a clear commitment, not a vague claim. The user should understand what "verified" means.
- **Why it matters:** Trust is the foundation of the platform. The verification commitment is what makes the platform trustworthy.

#### 2.1.5 Message 5: Independent

**"The platform is independent. We are not affiliated with any political party, candidate, or commercial interest. Our funding is disclosed in our quarterly transparency reports."**

- **When to use:** Every communication about the platform's governance. Every press inquiry about bias.
- **How to use:** As a clear commitment, backed by evidence (the transparency reports).
- **Why it matters:** Independence is what makes the platform credible. Without it, the polls are just political tools.

### 2.2 The Secondary Messages (Use as Needed)

These messages support the primary messages and are used in specific contexts.

#### 2.2.1 Security

**"Your data is encrypted, stored in Nigeria, and never sold. We are compliant with the Nigeria Data Protection Regulation (NDPR)."**

- **When to use:** Privacy-focused communications. Security incidents. NDPR-related questions.

#### 2.2.2 Transparency

**"We publish quarterly transparency reports detailing our funding, our activities, and our impact. The reports are public."**

- **When to use:** Funder communications. Press inquiries. Governance-focused communications.

#### 2.2.3 Accessibility

**"The platform is designed to be accessible to all users, including those with disabilities. We follow WCAG 2.1 AA standards."**

- **When to use:** Accessibility-focused communications. User feedback about accessibility issues.

#### 2.2.4 Local Language

**"Currently, the platform is in English. Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo, and Pidgin versions are planned for Year 2."**

- **When to use:** Communications with non-English speakers. Local language support inquiries.

#### 2.2.5 Pilot

**"The platform is currently in its pilot phase in Lagos, Nigeria. We plan to expand nationally in 2027–2028."**

- **When to use:** All communications during the pilot. The pilot status should be transparent, not hidden.

### 2.3 The Anti-Messages (What We Don't Say)

The platform does not make the following claims, even if they might be technically true. These are the boundaries.

| Don't say | Why not |
|----------|---------|
| "The platform can change government policy." | Polls are non-binding. Implying otherwise is misleading. |
| "The platform can affect elections." | Polls are non-binding. The platform has no electoral role. |
| "The platform is the only way to..." | There are many ways to engage with governance and the legal system. The platform is one tool among many. |
| "The platform is the best at..." | Subjective. Avoid superlatives that invite comparison and criticism. |
| "Your data is 100% safe." | No system is 100% safe. Use specific, verifiable claims instead. |
| "The platform guarantees..." | The platform facilitates; it doesn't guarantee. Avoid overpromising. |
| "Everyone is using the platform." | Hyperbole. Use specific, verifiable metrics instead. |
| "The platform is endorsed by [government official]." | Government endorsements compromise the platform's independence. The platform is independent, not government-affiliated. |
| "The platform is supported by [political party]." | Same as above. |
| "Lawyers on the platform are the best." | Subjective. Lawyers are verified (bar-checked), not endorsed. |
| "You should vote [specific option] on the next poll." | The platform is non-partisan. We do not advocate for any position. |

## 3. Voice and Tone

The platform's voice is the personality that comes through in every communication. It's consistent across all materials and channels.

### 3.1 The Voice Attributes

The platform's voice has these attributes:

| Attribute | What it means | What it doesn't mean |
|-----------|---------------|----------------------|
| **Warm** | Friendly, approachable, human | Casual, unprofessional, flippant |
| **Clear** | Plain language, direct, no jargon | Simplistic, condescending |
| **Empowering** | Encourages participation, emphasizes user agency | Pushy, preachy, moralizing |
| **Honest** | Admits limitations, acknowledges uncertainty | Negative, apologetic, defeatist |
| **Respectful** | Treats the user as an adult, respects their time | Patronizing, over-formal, cold |

### 3.2 The Tone Variations

The tone varies by context, but the voice stays consistent.

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| **Onboarding** | Welcoming, encouraging | "Welcome to the platform! Here's how to get started." |
| **Privacy** | Reassuring, specific | "Your votes are completely anonymous. We do not store your identity with your vote." |
| **Security incident** | Calm, transparent, accountable | "We're aware of the issue and are investigating. We'll update you within [time]." |
| **Feedback request** | Humble, specific | "We're sorry the experience didn't meet your expectations. Could you tell us what happened?" |
| **Press inquiry** | Professional, factual, responsive | "Thank you for your interest. Here's the information you requested. [Response]" |
| **Crisis** | Direct, accountable, action-oriented | "We made a mistake. Here's what happened, what we're doing, and what we'll change." |
| **Celebration** | Genuine, specific, not boastful | "Thanks to the pilot cohort, 500 citizens have participated in their first civic poll." |
| **Legal / compliance** | Formal, precise, careful | "Per the Nigeria Data Protection Regulation, you have the right to..." |

### 3.3 Words to Use and Avoid

This is the editorial guide for all marketing copy.

#### 3.3.1 Words to Use

| Use | Why |
|-----|-----|
| **Citizen** | Respectful, accurate |
| **You** / **your** | Direct, personal |
| **We** / **our** | Inclusive, accountable |
| **Free** | Clear, honest |
| **Verified** | Specific, verifiable |
| **Anonymous** | Clear, accurate |
| **Non-binding** | Specific, accurate |
| **Independent** | Clear, accurate |
| **Transparent** | Clear, accurate |
| **Free 15-minute consultation** | Specific, not vague |
| **Cryptographically verified** | Specific, technical-but-accessible |
| **Pilot** | Honest about the current state |
| **Quarterly transparency report** | Specific, verifiable |

#### 3.3.2 Words to Avoid

| Avoid | Why |
|-------|-----|
| **User** (instead of "citizen") | Impersonal, tech-bro |
| **Customers** / **clients** | Commercial framing; we're not a business |
| **Voters** (when referring to polls) | Implies electoral effect; polls are non-binding |
| **Dispute resolution** | Implies we're a court; we're not |
| **Legal services** | Implies we provide legal advice; we connect you with lawyers |
| **Simply** / **just** | Condescending |
| **Obviously** | Condescending |
| **Revolutionary** / **game-changing** | Hyperbole |
| **The best** / **world-class** / **industry-leading** | Subjective |
| **Guys** (informal address) | Unprofessional, gendered |
| **Revolution** | Hyperbole, political connotation |
| **Disrupt** | Tech-bro jargon |
| **Leverage** (as a verb) | Jargon |
| **Synergy** | Jargon |
| **Move the needle** | Cliché |
| **Game-changer** | Hyperbole |
| **Cutting-edge** | Hyperbole |

#### 3.3.3 Phrases to Avoid

These specific phrases are problematic:

| Avoid | Why | Use instead |
|-------|-----|-------------|
| "We believe in democracy" | Platitude | "We're building tools that complement democratic participation" |
| "Empowering citizens to..." | Overused, vague | "Giving citizens tools to..." or simply "Helping citizens..." |
| "The future of civic engagement" | Hyperbole | "A tool for civic engagement" |
| "Revolutionary platform" | Hyperbole | "An independent platform" |
| "The best way to..." | Subjective | "One way to..." |
| "Your vote counts" | Implies electoral effect | "Your voice matters" or "Your view is recorded" |
| "Guaranteed to..." | Overpromising | "Designed to..." |
| "We don't sell your data" (without context) | Sounds defensive | "Your data is never sold to third parties. We are funded by grants, not by data sales." |
| "Join the movement" | Political connotation | "Join the platform" or "Become a verified citizen" |

## 4. Audiences

The platform communicates to several distinct audiences, each with different needs and messages.

### 4.1 The Primary Audiences

These are the audiences the platform is designed to serve.

#### 4.1.1 Citizens (the Primary Audience)

**Who they are:** Nigerian citizens, 18+, with a smartphone or computer and internet access.

**What they care about:**
- Their voice matters (Pillar 1)
- Their evidence is protected (Pillar 2)
- Legal help is accessible (Pillar 3)
- Their privacy is protected (Non-binding, Anonymous, Free messages)
- The platform is trustworthy (Verified, Independent messages)

**How to reach them:**
- Social media (Twitter/X, Facebook, Instagram)
- Community organizations and NGOs
- Civil society groups
- Word of mouth (especially in the pilot)
- Local language outreach (Year 2)

**What to emphasize:**
- Non-binding (polls are citizen sentiment, not legal action)
- Anonymous (votes are private)
- Free (the platform and the lawyer consultation)
- Verified (lawyers, evidence, results)
- Independent (no political or commercial affiliation)

**What to avoid:**
- Implying that polls affect elections or policy
- Implying that the platform provides legal advice
- Implying that the platform is government-affiliated
- Overpromising ("the best", "guaranteed", "revolutionary")

#### 4.1.2 Lawyers (a Critical Audience)

**Who they are:** Practicing lawyers in Nigeria, especially in Lagos during the pilot.

**What they care about:**
- New clients (without the overhead of marketing)
- Fair compensation (the flat subscription, not a percentage of fees)
- Professional standing (the platform's bar verification maintains standards)
- Efficient matching (the algorithm matches cases to practice areas)

**How to reach them:**
- Nigerian Bar Association chapters
- Law firm partnerships
- Legal publications and conferences
- Direct outreach by the Operations team

**What to emphasize:**
- Flat subscription (not a percentage of fees)
- Bar verification maintains standards
- Matching is transparent (the score breakdown)
- Free consultation is funded by the platform (not by the lawyer)
- Engagement is outside the platform (no interference)

**What to avoid:**
- Implying the platform is a "gig economy" for lawyers
- Implying the platform is a "lead generation" tool
- Implying that the platform interferes with lawyer-client relationships
- Overpromising case volume

#### 4.1.3 Civil Society (a Key Audience)

**Who they are:** NGOs, civic organizations, professional associations, community groups.

**What they care about:**
- Civic engagement tools
- Transparency and accountability
- Access to justice
- Non-partisan independence

**How to reach them:**
- Direct partnerships
- Events and conferences
- Joint programs
- Co-authored content

**What to emphasize:**
- The platform is independent (not government-affiliated)
- The Advisory Board provides independent review
- The transparency reports are public
- The platform complements (not replaces) their work

**What to avoid:**
- Competing with civil society organizations
- Implying that the platform is a substitute for their advocacy
- Implying that the platform is politically neutral but secretly aligned

### 4.2 The Secondary Audiences

These audiences are important but not the primary focus.

#### 4.2.1 Press and Media

**Who they are:** Journalists, bloggers, influencers, media organizations.

**What they care about:**
- A compelling story (the platform's mission)
- Transparency (access to information, interviews)
- Timeliness (news hooks)
- Independence (no strings attached)

**How to reach them:**
- Press releases
- Press kit
- Direct outreach by the Project Sponsor
- The status page and the transparency reports

**What to emphasize:**
- The mission (citizen empowerment, evidence integrity, access to justice)
- The independence (no political or commercial affiliation)
- The transparency (quarterly reports, public methodology)
- The pilot status (honest, specific, not overpromising)

**What to avoid:**
- Exclusives or favoritism (we serve all press equally)
- Speculation (we don't speculate about future features or outcomes)
- Endorsements (we don't endorse political positions, and we don't accept endorsements from political parties)

#### 4.2.2 Donors and Funders

**Who they are:** Foundations, philanthropic organizations, government grants, individual donors.

**What they care about:**
- Impact (measurable outcomes)
- Sustainability (path to financial independence)
- Transparency (clear reporting)
- Alignment with their mission

**How to reach them:**
- Grant applications
- Direct outreach by the Project Sponsor
- The transparency reports
- The website's "Support Us" page

**What to emphasize:**
- The measurable impact (specific metrics, not vague claims)
- The path to sustainability (the business case)
- The transparency (quarterly reports, audit log)
- The alignment with funder missions (civic engagement, access to justice)

**What to avoid:**
- Vague impact claims ("thousands of citizens empowered")
- Unsustainable promises ("we'll be self-sustaining by Year 2" — too aggressive)
- Overhead criticism (we acknowledge the cost of running the platform)
- Political alignment (we don't accept funding from political parties)

#### 4.2.3 Regulators and Government

**Who they are:** NDPC, NBA, other regulatory bodies.

**What they care about:**
- Compliance (NDPR, NBA rules, etc.)
- Transparency (clear reporting, audit trail)
- Cooperation (we work with regulators, not against them)
- Independence (we're not seeking government control)

**How to reach them:**
- Direct engagement by the Legal Director
- Compliance reports
- The transparency reports
- The audit log (accessible on request)

**What to emphasize:**
- The compliance posture (per [../technical/Security.md §9](../technical/Security.md#9-compliance))
- The cooperation (we welcome regulatory review)
- The independence (we're not seeking government control)
- The transparency (our decisions are documented and auditable)

**What to avoid:**
- Defensive language (we're not adversarial; we're cooperative)
- Overpromising (we don't promise "zero risk" — no system can)
- Criticism of regulators (we respect the regulatory framework)

### 4.3 The Audience-to-Message Map

|----------|-----------------|--------------------|-------|
| Citizens | Non-binding, Anonymous, Free | Independent, Verified | Implying electoral effect |
| Lawyers | Flat subscription, Bar-verified | Matching is transparent, Engagement is private | Implying gig economy or lead gen |

| Donors | Impact, Sustainability | Transparency, Alignment | Vague claims, political alignment |
| Regulators | Compliance, Cooperation | Transparency, Independence | Defensive language, overpromising |

## 5. The Review Checklist

Every marketing material (website copy, press release, newsletter, social media post, presentation) should be reviewed against this checklist before publication.

### 5.1 The Content Checklist

- [ ] **The non-binding disclaimer is prominent** (for any mention of polls)
- [ ] **The anonymity commitment is clear** (for any mention of voting)
- [ ] **The free commitment is clear** (for any mention of the lawyer consultation)
- [ ] **The verified commitment is clear** (for any mention of lawyers, evidence, or results)
- [ ] **The independent commitment is clear** (for any mention of governance or funding)
- [ ] **The pilot status is transparent** (during the pilot)
- [ ] **No overpromising** ("the best", "guaranteed", "revolutionary")
- [ ] **No political alignment** (no endorsement of parties, candidates, or positions)
- [ ] **No "user" or "customer"** (use "citizen")
- [ ] **No jargon** ("leverage", "synergy", "disrupt", "game-changer")
- [ ] **No "simply" or "just"** (condescending)
- [ ] **The contact information is included** (where appropriate)
- [ ] **The links to the User Guide, FAQ, and Troubleshooting are included** (for user-facing materials)

### 5.2 The Accessibility Checklist

- [ ] **The text is readable** (plain language, short sentences, short paragraphs)
- [ ] **The headings are clear** (descriptive, scannable)
- [ ] **The images have alt text** (for screen readers)
- [ ] **The links are descriptive** (not "click here")
- [ ] **The color contrast is sufficient** (WCAG 2.1 AA)
- [ ] **The content is navigable by keyboard** (for interactive materials)
- [ ] **The content is available in a plain text format** (for screen readers)

### 5.3 The Legal Review Checklist

- [ ] **The Legal Director has reviewed** the material (for press releases, legal content, or anything that makes commitments)
- [ ] **The non-binding disclaimer is legally sufficient** (the wording matches the platform's commitment)
- [ ] **No false or misleading claims** (per the legal review)
- [ ] **No defamation** (per the legal review)
- [ ] **No copyright violations** (per the legal review)
- [ ] **The privacy implications are reviewed** (per NDPR)

## 6. The Marketing Channels

- The home page (the value proposition, the call to action)
- The about page (the mission, the team, the governance)

### 6.5 Events and Conferences

The platform participates in events and conferences that are relevant to its mission:
- Civic technology conferences
- Legal aid and access to justice events
- Civil society gatherings
- Academic conferences on civic engagement

We do not participate in partisan political events.

## 7. The Brand Guidelines

The platform's brand is more than the logo. It's the experience, the tone, and the trust.

### 7.1 The Logo

The logo is a simple mark that represents the bridge between citizens and institutions. The logo should be used in its full form (with the wordmark) for most purposes. The icon-only form is for small spaces (e.g., favicons, social media avatars).

The logo should always be used:
- In the correct colors (specified in the brand assets)
- With sufficient clear space (at least the height of the mark on all sides)
- Without distortion, rotation, or modification

The logo should never be used:
- In colors other than the specified brand colors
- On backgrounds that compromise legibility
- In a way that suggests endorsement by a third party

### 7.2 The Colors

The platform's color palette is specified in the brand assets. The primary colors are:
- **Brand primary:** used for the logo, key buttons, and emphasis
- **Brand secondary:** used for secondary buttons and supporting elements
- **Surface colors:** used for backgrounds and surfaces
- **Text colors:** used for body text, headings, and captions
- **Status colors:** used for verified (green), flagged (yellow), and not-verified (red) states

The non-binding disclaimer uses a distinct color (a muted amber) to avoid the implication of an alert or warning.

### 7.3 The Typography

The platform uses a consistent typography across all materials. The type scale is specified in the brand assets.

### 7.4 The Imagery

Imagery should be:
- Authentic (real people, real situations, with consent)
- Diverse (reflecting the diversity of Nigeria)
- Respectful (no stereotypes, no condescension)
- Relevant (directly related to the platform's mission)

Imagery should never be:
- Stock photography (it looks generic and inauthentic)
- Political imagery (party logos, candidates, partisan symbols)
- Deceptive (misrepresenting the platform or its users)

## 8. The Crisis Communications Plan

In a crisis (security incident, regulatory action, public controversy), the platform follows a specific communications plan.

### 8.1 The Crisis Communication Team

The crisis communication team is:
- **Project Sponsor:** the lead spokesperson
- **Legal Director:** the legal review
- **Operations Director:** the operational coordination
- **Engineering Lead:** the technical response

The Project Sponsor is the only authorized spokesperson during a crisis. No one else speaks to the press or the public without explicit approval.

### 8.2 The Crisis Communication Process

1. **Detect:** the crisis is detected (alert, user report, press inquiry)
2. **Triage:** the Project Sponsor assesses the severity and decides on the response
3. **Investigate:** the relevant team investigates (Engineering for technical, Legal for legal, Operations for operational)
4. **Prepare:** the Project Sponsor prepares the communication (with Legal review)
5. **Communicate:** the communication is sent (initial response within the SLA, updates as needed)
6. **Resolve:** the underlying issue is resolved
7. **Post-mortem:** a blameless post-mortem is written
8. **Follow-up:** the post-crisis communication is sent

### 8.3 The Crisis Communication Templates

The crisis communication templates are in [../operations/Runbooks.md §5](../operations/Runbooks.md#5-communication-templates). The most relevant for marketing:

- **P1 platform down** (internal + external)
- **P1 security incident** (internal + Legal + Board)
- **P1 database failure** (internal)
- **Moderation controversy** (external, with Legal review)
- **Regulatory action** (external, with Legal review)

## Appendix A: The Voice Cheat Sheet

A one-page reference for anyone writing marketing copy.

### A.1 The Voice in One Sentence

**"We're a warm, clear, empowering, honest, and respectful platform that helps Nigerian citizens have a voice, protect their evidence, and access legal help."**

### A.2 The Top 5 Messages (Always Include)

1. All polls are non-binding
2. Your votes are anonymous
3. The platform is free for citizens
4. Everything is verified (lawyers, evidence, results)
5. The platform is independent (no political or commercial affiliation)

### A.3 The Top 5 Words to Avoid

1. "Simply" / "just" (condescending)
2. "User" / "customer" (use "citizen")
3. "Revolutionary" / "game-changing" (hyperbole)
4. "Guaranteed" / "the best" (overpromising)
5. "Voter" (implies electoral effect; use "citizen" or "participant")

### A.4 The Anti-Messages (Never Say)

- "The platform can change government policy."
- "The platform can affect elections."
- "Your vote counts" (implies electoral effect; say "your view is recorded" or "your voice matters")
- "Everyone is using the platform."
- "The platform is endorsed by [government official]."

- **Press:** press@najiacommunitybridge.com
